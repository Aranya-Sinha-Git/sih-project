Brief on the Blowout Incident

Brief on the Blowout Incident

A blowout is the uncontrolled release of crude oil and/or natural gas along with condensate from an oil well or gas well after pressure control systems have failed. It can happen during drilling of the well or during workover operation.

To avoid the blowout in oil/gas wells, there are barriers which are used to prevent the uncontrolled flow of crude oil/ natural gas from the reservoirs.

During drilling operations the pressure of the reservoirs are generally contained with the help of hydrostatic head of the drilling mud which is termed as primary barrier whose mud column weight will exert pressure higher than the reservoir’s pressure. But sometimes because of some reasons, like loss of drilling mud in the reservoir, the pressure exerted by the weight of mud becomes less than the reservoir pressure resulting in internal kick/blowout and if no action is taken then the flow of oil/gas from the reservoir will start to push the mud out to surface thus initiating a blowout. In this case a second barrier called secondary barrier-a mechanical equipment called Blowout Preventer (BOP) comes into play which is always already installed on the surface above the well. This BOP is closed in that situation thus preventing the blowout. But in case of failure of both the barriers primary and secondary, then there will be a possibility of occurrence of blowout which is to be handled through specialised well control equipment and experts.

Time taken to control the blowout depends on the severity of the blowout, geographical locations, availability of water sources, mobilization period of equipment and services etc. A Few blowout incident occurred worldwide is listed below:

Brief on Blowout at Baghjan Well No. 05 (BGN-05)

Brief on Blowout at Baghjan Well No. 05 (BGN-05)

The Baghjan Oil & Gas field of Oil India Limited (OIL) is located in Hapjan, Kakopather, Guijan and Itakhuli Revenue Blocks of Doomdooma sub-district, Tinsukia district, Assam. OIL discovered this field in 2003 after drilling its first exploratory Well ‘Baghjan-1’. The Baghjan field development and commercial production started from December 2005 after successful completion of the Well ‘Baghjan-2’.

The Baghjan Well-5 (BGN-5) was successfully drilled by OIL in 2006 and completed in 2007 but due to non-availability of facility at site, actual production of gas and condensate started in September 2015.

It was observed that the BGN-5 well was producing less gas from Gas Cap of Baghjan field, which might have led to faster reservoir pressure drop resulting lesser recovery. To enhance the production, Work Over operation was carried out by a drilling subcontractor M/s John Energy Ltd. and activity started on April 19, 2020 on BGN-5. While workover operations were ongoing to produce gas from a new sand, the blowout occurred on May 27 around 10.30 am;. The well caught fire on June 09, 2020 around 1.30 pm and continued till November 15, 2020.

With the engagement of international experts from M/s ALERT, CMT team of ONGCL and OIL’s own experts, the well was capped successfully.

Caring for affected people

Caring for affected people

Relief & Rehabilitation

  • Around 1610 families of nearby areas were evacuated for their safety and accommodated in 12 nos. of Relief Camps with the assistance from District Administration.
  • Arrangement for fooding & lodging, lighting, hygiene, toilet, drinking water and medical requirements were made with the help of District Administration and Local Organisations/Associations.
  • Relief work at all relief camps were closely monitored by individual officer of OIL assigned to respective camps.
  • OIL’s hospital was also extending services for treatment for routine ailments. Specialist doctors visited the camps time to time.
  • Food and medicines for livestock’swere also arranged with the help of government agencies.
  • Government organizations like Anganwadi workers, ASHA karmi were extending their support in the relief camps.
  • OIL provided one light vehicle for each camp and also provided 7x24 hours ambulances for the camps. Health camps were setup at each camp by OIL and District Authority. Indian Army also supported by providing a team of Medical experts. A 7x24 hours central Medical camp was set up at Baghjan EPS. Separate fleet of Ambulances were kept at this camp to send the referral patient to OIL hospital as well as other referral Hospitals as per need.
  • All relief camps have been closed.

Assessment of Damage

District Administration constituted a committee for assessment of property loss for which OIL has to deposit the compensation amount to District Administration for disbursal to the affected families.

Caring for Environment

Caring for Environment

OIL has left no stone unturned to mitigate the impacts of the blow out and the subsequent fire in the vicinity of the well BGN#5.

OIL has carried out Subsidence Study to assess the impact around the upper Assam Oil Field area of OIL including Dibru-Saikhowa NP. The said study was carried out during 2017-18 by engaging M/S National Remote Sensing Centre (NSRC), Hyderabad, an unit of Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO), GoI, and the report established that there is no remarkable subsidence around the Oil Fields of OIL and whatever mitigation measures proposed are already under process of implementation.

As a responsible E&P Operator, OIL restored 30-35 ha lands by completing plantation of 64,500 trees by August, 2019 with the help of Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR)- North East Institute of Science and Technology (NEIST), Jorhat, with aim of bringing back lands to its original conditions in abandoned well sites at seven locations. OIL has also assigned job to CSIR-NEIST for upbringing the planted trees and to develop natural jungle in the said areas within a span of three years up to 31.01.2023.

Post Incident Efforts: Efforts have been undertaken by OIL by engaging its own resources and hired services from organizations of national repute to minimize further impacts on the environment and to recuperate from the damage caused to the environment. Internally, picking up of thin film of floating condensate oil was carried out by OIL. Sorbents were used to contain the spread of condensate oil. The following organizations are involved for the purpose mentioned above, apart from the District Administration.

  • M/s ERM India Pvt. Limited

  • The Energy Research Institute (TERI)

  • Assam Agricultural University

  • CSIR- North East Institute of Science and Technology

  • Indian Institute of Technology (IIT), Guwahati

OIL always takes care of environment wherever it operates. From the moment of the blowout incident, OIL was taking all necessary care to pick up deposited condensate around the affected area. Water accumulated due to continuous spraying to the well was collected in a pit and was disposed after treatment. OIL took following initiatives for assessing environmental impact of the blowout and fire incident and its remedial measures:

The actions for salvage planned and initiated for execution by OIL to mitigate the impacts on the environment is briefed below :

Regular monitoring of Air quality, Water quality, Noise level & presence of Gas (Lower Explosive Limit): The main objective of the proposed study is to see the impact of the fire on ambient air quality in the vicinity of the well. Specific objectives of the study are:

  • To measure the levels of PM2.5, SO2, NOx, methane, non-methane hydrocarbons (NMHC), total hydrocarbons (TH) and total volatile organic compounds (TVOCs) at a radius of 0.5 km, 1 km, 1.5 km and 2 km from the impacted well head in all the four directions on a rotational basis.

  • To measure the sound levels (dBA) at different locations from the well head.

  • To submit a report on the monitoring carried out and infer upon the monitored status with the prescribed standard limits for the various parameter, wherever applicable.

The sampling and monitoring techniques are shown in table below.

Environmental Impact Assessment: OIL engaged M/s ERM India Pvt. Limited, a NABET accredited consultant for assessment of impact on affected area - land and water bodies, onsite observation of impacted environmental attributes – air, noise, soil, surface and ground water resources, observation of impacted forest, vegetation, tea garden, terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, assess the potential for community exposure, health and safety aspects in terms of exposure of local communities.

OIL engaged TERI to study the impact of the fire on ambient air quality in the vicinity of the well.

TERI, New Delhi carried out monitoring of air quality parameters as per CPCB and other applicable guidelines. Based on the data collected and analysis thereof, the ambient air quality parameters for PM2.5, SO2, NOx, methane, non-methane hydrocarbons (NMHC), total hydrocarbons (TH) and total volatile organic compounds (TVOCs) were well below the prescribed statutory limits. The results of PM2.5, SO2 and NO2 concentrations measured at different locations from the fire point were found to be well within the NAAQS prescribed by CPCB >

OIL engaged TERI to carry out bioremediation of sludge in and around BGN# 5 well using a technology developed in-house by R&D Department of OIL. The In situ- bioremediation jobs include supply and application of Biomaterial, tilling of contaminated soil at regular intervals, arranging fresh soil to mix with sludge during biomaterial application, sample collection, sample testing, reporting of treatment progress at regular intervals in treated sample. Accordingly bio remediation jobs has been completed.

Seismological and Geophysical Study at the Baghjan Well no.5 blowout site: A team from Council of Scientific & Industrial Research – North East Institute of Science &Technology, Jorhat carried out tremor study using seismological technique under the guidance of State Govt. The satellite data (InSAR) analysis do not show any surface deformation at the vicinity of the blow-out site.

IIT- G Study on Thermal mapping and Sound characterizes: Thermal Mapping of Structures using Thermal Imaging Camera: This study was carried out to understand the causes of the cracks that were reported in many houses in the area surrounding Baghjan well no. 5 blowout site. Infrared photographs of the cracks were taken using thermal imaging camera and analyzed using specialized processing software at IIT Guwahati, for this purpose.

Following are the summary of the findings submitted by IITG:

  1. Sound Intensity Measurement Inference: The sound time history with high pressure intensity in dB emitting at low occurrence rate is not detrimental to the structural integrity.
  2. Spectral Energy Inference: Overall, the sound intensity is not too intense to cause any detrimental effect on structural integrity.
  3. Frequency Inference: The sound pressure intensities from the gas blowout has no significant influence on formation of cracks in the buildings.
  4. Thermal Measurement Inference: Based on the thermal maps, it is concluded that there is no significant effect of fire in blowout well in Baghjan well no. 5 on the formation of crack in the structures.

Challenges

Challenges

  1. Inclement weather condition for a prolong period hampered the progress of civil work mainly in developing approach roads, placement area for engines, pumps etc.

  2. The impending monsoon and flood thereafter was causing major setbacks with disruption of land communication by washing away a bridge for planned operation. Anticipating higher ground water level due to monsoon and limitation for evacuation of water around the wellhead pit,the Plan for cutting the wellhead to install well capping head had to be abandoned.

  3. The ubiquitous threat of infection from COVID-19 in site personnel was a cause of concern for all and the mandatory covid-19 protocols affected the men & material movement. The Core team members including CMD, Directors and RCE got infected by covid-19 in the midway of the operation.

Post Blowout Period

Post Blowout Period

The BGN-5 well was successfully killed on November 15, 2020. It took 2-3 weeks time to remove the machineries and equipment used to control the blowout from the site.

ERM team conducted the environmental study form December 09 to 18, 2020. The environmental context during post blowout period as observed at site during December 09 to 18,2020 is presented below.

  • The BGN-5 well site was capped and security guard posted. The machineries and equipment were removed from the site.
  • As the well (BGN-5) was killed, no light, sound or release of gas and condensate observed from the BGN-5 well.
  • The waste pits (4 numbers), constructed during drilling activity, located on the western boundary of the BGN-5 well pad, were used to store the runoff water from well pad area during blowout control phase. Floating oil layer was observed on water, bank soil of these pits.
  • Five additional ponds were created along the Western, South-western and North-eastern sides of the BGN-5 well to store river water during well killing operation. Visible oil deposition was not observed in the ponds.

Fire affected area - visual observations as of December 2020 showed the following
Beyond 200 m radius, clear signs of re-growth of vegetation including grasslands were noted. During the ecological assessment of December (2020), it was observed that most of the vegetation including grasslands, herbs and shrubs had grown back starting from 100-200 m away from the BGN-5.

Visual observations are as follows

  • Regeneration/ regrowth of grassland vegetation was observed in the condensate-affected area.
  • Tress in this zone were also regenerated; however, some sissoo (Dalbergia sissoo) plants were not fully regenerated.
  • Agricultural activity was observed at the MMGL area in proximity to the BGN-5 well site. Cultivation of paddy, tomato, French beans, potato, pulses etc. was observed in the MMGL areas; however, the first crops were yet to be harvested during the time of post blowout survey period.
  • Discussion held with local villagers revealed that the pulses cultivated during and post blowout period showed very high vegetative growths

Fishing activities were observed in the MMB and Dangori River during December 2020. Jeng or katal fishing is observed mostly in the wetlands. A specific portion of the water body selected to set up the trap by duping tree branches along with the water hyacinth. Actually, a shed was prepared for the fishes that look like a bed of water hyacinth. This bed was surrounded by long bamboo rods to prevent movement of free-floating hyacinths. This set up is usually left for 2-3 months. The consultation with fishermen revealed that fishing from Jeng are carried out during February and March. It was also observed fishermen also carried out the capture fishing through different types of fishing gears like Bhor jal (Dip net), Dheki jal (Lift net), Fansi jal (Gill net), `Phelni (Triangular basket net), Khewali jal, Kapda jal (Mosquito net), traps, etc. Fishermen also carried out fishing the waterbody/ low-lying area of DSNP and Dangori River.

The MMB is an important habitat for migratory birds. The area has also been declared as an Important Bird Area (IBA) and Endemic Bird Area with some rare and highly range-restricted species. During field visit, it was observed that migratory birds came into the MMB as well as in the Dangori River – exposed riverbed area.

Honouring Our Hero: OIL's Commitment to Safety and Remembrance

Honouring Our Hero: OIL's Commitment to Safety and Remembrance

A Tribute to Sacrifice:

OIL remembers with immense pride and deep sorrow the energy warrior who lost his life in the line of duty during the Baghjan blowout - Sri Arnab Kishor Bordoloi (electrical engineer).

Remembering Arnab Kishor Bordoloi:

Recognizing its commitment to its employees, OIL has provided financial support to Arnab's family, following company policy. We've also explored opportunities for compassionate employment to offer further assistance. While Arnab's father is a retired government employee and his younger sister is still pursuing her further studies, OIL remains dedicated to supporting the family in every way possible. This includes extending medical benefits and honouring Arnab's legacy through:

  1. Arnab Kishor Bordoloi Memorial Award: To commemorate Arnab Kishor Bordoloi's sacrifice, OIL has established an annual award for the best graduating engineer at Jorhat Engineering College, his alma mater.

  2. Jorhat Engineering College Auditorium: As part of its Diamond Jubilee celebrations, OIL funded the construction of a state-of-the-art auditorium at Jorhat Engineering College, named after Arnab Kishor Bordoloi.

  3. Substation Dedication: A substation at our FHQ in Duliajan is named in honor of Arnab.

  4. Annual Safety Programs: In Arnab's memory, we conduct various annual HSE (Health, Safety, and Environment) awareness and technical sessions. His sacrifice continues to inspire a strong safety culture within OIL.

Enhancing HSE Measures:

Following the Baghjan incident, OIL has taken significant steps to strengthen its HSE (Health, Safety, and Environment) management system:

  • Improved Procedures: Comprehensive review and revision of 1044 Drilling and Workover Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), Job Safety Analysis (JSA), and safety-related policies.

  • Organizational Restructuring: Clear demarcation of responsibilities for workover operations, including top management accountability. Modified mines structure for smoother operations.

  • Crisis Management Group: A strengthened Crisis Management Team (CMT) equipped with critical well control equipment and additional manpower.

  • Digitalization: Workflow-based work order program preparation and an HSE incident reporting system for improved monitoring.

  • Intensified Training & Audits: Increased frequency of safety audits, drills, workshops, webinars, and training programs to bolster safety awareness and preparedness.
